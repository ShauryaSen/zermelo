## 2. The noise construction

The truth is the record and stays the record; only the forecast moves.

```
truth    = ERA5                     unchanged at every σ
forecast = truth − n(σ)             what the agent is told, and the belief's prior mean
error    = truth − forecast = n(σ)  what the world carries
W        = forecast + error = ERA5  exactly, at every σ
```

Selected by `problem.forecast = "noisy"`, beside your `"GEFS"` and `"gaussian"`. The draw folds in its own
key (`0xF0EC`) so no existing sweep's random stream changes. This gives Sarah what she asked for — the same
ground truth on every line, the noise only in the forecast — by construction rather than by averaging.

Scoring is navigation alone: `resource_weight = 0`, so an altitude change costs the plan nothing. The world
still caps changes by `resource_units`, and it binds: 36 of 40 spent in a typical episode.

## 3. Three things in the existing code that look wrong to me

I have not changed any of them. Each is a case of something assuming behaviour the world does not have.

**The belief's amplitude does not match this record.** `amplitude=2.4` is annotated "the RMS of W - F in this
record". Measured on `era5_npac_wide.npz` that RMS is **2.797** in `u` and **2.784** in `v`. The config is
still named `ERA5_SH_JUL2021`, so 2.4 looks measured on the Southern Hemisphere record and carried over. The
belief is then ~16% overconfident in amplitude, ~35% in variance, before any noise is added. It is the same
at every σ, so it shifts the lines together rather than tilting the trend.

**The belief's altitude lengthscale fights the error model.** The spatial kernel uses 3.0 levels, which
assumes the forecast error is coherent up the column. `GaussianError` draws independently per altitude. So
the belief is mis-specified in exactly the direction a balloon steers, since altitude is its only lever. I
left it alone deliberately — a well-specified belief would make the proxy and true bounds coincide — but it
should be a choice, not an accident.

**The planner thinks everything is reachable.** The diagnostics report `reachable = 1.000` exactly: the
value-iteration table puts every candidate inside `L = 20` steps, including one **6 500 km** away. A leg has
60 h, and at the fastest wind the episode actually flew (17 m/s) that is about 3 700 km. The acquisition
prices waypoints with that table, so the chooser sends the balloon after targets it has no range for. Seed 2
spent 40 moves sitting at a constant 6 500 km from its waypoint. This is upstream of everything the noise
sweep measures.

## 4. What I changed, and why

**The forecast column is persistence, confirmed exactly.** `max |forecast[t] − truth[t−8]| = 0.0`. The
"GEFS" mode name is a mode name; the file's contents are ERA5 shifted 24 h. That settles the meeting's
open question.

**Scoring.** The tables reported arrival and steps walked, but not the summed distance to the waypoint,
which is what the MPC minimises. Added, computed after the fact from the record — the positions, altitudes
and standing waypoint it already stores — so no simulation code is touched and every existing number is
unchanged. `χ` places a state in km as the chord on the sphere then the altitude, matching `SphereGrid.embed`
and `Steps.embed`.

**But the absolute distance is the wrong number to read, and the fix matters.** At σ = 2.4 it measured
88 445 km with a standard deviation of 100 308 — wider than its own mean, because the waypoint chooser is
unconstrained and a seed handed a far target accumulates distance however well it flies. Worse for a sweep:
the forecast is what the belief is built on, so **σ changes which waypoints get chosen**, and an absolute
distance would confound "flew worse" with "was sent further". The headline is now `gap closed`,
`1 − d_j(k_j)/d_j(1)` per leg: scale-free, bounded by 1, and it answers the question actually being asked.

**Only legs with something to fly are scored**, `F = { j : d_j(1) > ρ }`. Two kinds are excluded:

- *Legs that never flew.* The acquisition sometimes picks a waypoint already inside the arrival radius, so
  the leg ends after one move having gone nowhere — and counts as an arrival. Seed 0's first leg: one move,
  143 km to 143 km. Restricting dropped arrival from **0.477 to 0.388**. Every arrival rate reported before
  this is inflated the same way, including in earlier sweeps.
- *Legs that could not have arrived.* Flagged as `out of reach`, 6% of legs at σ = 2.4.

**Seeds 8 → 32.** An episode finishes only ~4 legs, so arrival is a mean over four binary draws; its spread
over eight seeds left a standard error of 0.12, wider than the gap between neighbouring rungs.

**Fixes to my own work along the way**, for the record: `dataclasses.replace` on `NAVIGATOR` (it is a hydra
`_target_` dict, not a dataclass); `lat`/`lon` instead of the record's `latitude`/`longitude`; and a reach
bound built from the record's own maximum wind, which is a 60 m/s jet at 14.8 km and made the bound vacuous
at 13 000 km — a balloon holding a level cannot draw on a wind at another one.

## 5. The bounds are held back

`V_plan ≤ V_mpc ≤ V_hind` is a statement about a plan, a policy and hindsight **on one task**. The three arms
differ in their belief — `hind` is told the true field, `plan` averages 32 draws — and each chooses its
waypoint off its own belief, so each flies to a different sequence of targets. Their ratio is not a bound.
The objection applies to the arrival rate too, so it is not an artefact of how navigation is scored.

A bound needs **one waypoint sequence shared by all three arms**, so the navigator is the only thing that
differs. That is a change to how an arm is composed, not a sweep. The arms are written and left unregistered
in `sweeps/bounds_noise.py`, ready for it. `OpenLoopAgent` (solves once a leg) is in
`methods/waypoint_bo/agent.py` and works.

## 7. Still open

1. Open loop is once a leg, not once an episode. A leg is the natural unit because waypoints are rechosen.
2. `BELIEF_TRACKS_SIGMA = False` — the belief keeps its 2.4 amplitude as the real error grows, so it becomes
   overconfident. `True` recalibrates at every level. This changes what the experiment measures.
3. The hindsight horizon: matched to the 20-move budget, the whole 80-move episode, or both.
4. `n_winds = 32` for the floor, chosen large rather than tuned.
5. How the three arms come to share a waypoint sequence, which is what §5 waits on.
